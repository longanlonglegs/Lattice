Our results show that temperature is the dominant factor controlling vitamin C loss in stored orange juice. Juice kept at 40 degC lost more than five times as much vitamin C in a week as juice kept in the fridge.

Refrigeration at 4 degC reduced vitamin C loss by roughly two-thirds compared with storage at room temperature. This agrees with published work showing that cold storage is the most effective single measure for preserving vitamin C.

The loss of vitamin C followed first-order kinetics throughout the storage period, so a single rate constant can be used to predict how long juice keeps its vitamin C.

The DCPIP titration method gave accurate and reliable results for orange juice, so our measurements can be compared directly with studies that used HPLC.

The anomalous repeat run is best explained by contamination with copper from tap water, which is known to catalyse the oxidation of vitamin C.

Manufacturers should therefore prioritise cold storage over oxygen-barrier packaging if they want to protect the vitamin C content of juice.
