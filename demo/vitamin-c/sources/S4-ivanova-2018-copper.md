# Trace copper as a hidden catalyst in vitamin C stability tests

**Maria Ivanova** · *Demo Letters in Analytical Chemistry* 9 (2018) 120–123

> FICTIONAL SOURCE — written for testing the Lattice app. Not a real paper.

## Summary

Copper(II) and iron(III) ions catalyse the oxidation of ascorbic acid by oxygen. In this short communication we show how little copper is needed to distort a vitamin C stability experiment.

## What we did

Orange juice was stored in sealed glass vials at 4 °C and 20 °C for 7 days. Some vials were spiked with copper(II) sulfate to give 0.1, 0.5 or 1.0 mg/L of copper. Ascorbic acid was measured by HPLC.

## Findings

Adding just 0.5 mg/L of copper increased the rate of vitamin C loss by a factor of three to five at both temperatures. At this level, the effect of copper was larger than the effect of raising the storage temperature from 4 °C to 20 °C. Copper concentrations this high are easily reached when glassware is rinsed with tap water from copper pipes, or when juice is in contact with brass fittings.

## Recommendations

Vitamin C stability studies should use acid-washed glassware and deionised water for all rinsing. Unexpectedly high losses in one batch of samples should prompt a check for metal contamination before they are interpreted as a temperature or storage effect.
